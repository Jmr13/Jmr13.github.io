## Apply

I am not counting all the days when I had applied to numerous job postings. Counting them would take a toll on my mental and physical health. This is the first time I've experienced fatigue simply from filling out questionnaires, completing forms, and recording myself answering questions that I am obliged to answer just to move my application forward to recruiters. Everything you can do to stand out is a hard requirement now. It is awful. And the worst part? You can do everything right and still hear nothing back.

## Advent of AI interviews

Getting an invitation from a recruiter gives you a slight glimmer of hope that you can hardly imagine in this job market. For a brief moment, you feel seen. You feel like all those applications, all those hours spent tailoring your resume, and all that effort might finally amount to something. But your energy collapses in an instant when you realize you've been invited to an AI interview.

I don't hate AI interviews. Even though I am not good at interviews, I don't want to feel like I would be wasting someone's time. I understand the need for efficiency. I understand that recruiters have hundreds, perhaps thousands, of applications to sift through. But why do companies hate people so much? Or, to be more precise, why are we losing our humanity?

When did finding a job become a process where you must prove your worth to both a machine and a person?

## Cognitive downfall

It is estimated that today's frontier AI models are on the same level as someone with a PhD. But I doubt that comparison, especially when these models are trained to be generalists rather than specialists. When all knowledge is contained in one system, it risks becoming diluted, losing the nuances and semantics along the way, 

The moment you speak to AI, it will answer with apparent confidence and immediate context. Prolonged conversations, however, can lead to misaligned communication. Even with an enormous context window, the patterns and biases embedded in its training data can influence the direction and flow of a conversation. It's as if there are multiple paths laid out before it, and AI can wander down any of them, sometimes taking a detour so subtle that you don't realize you've lost the original point until you're already miles away.

## Perspective

Time is precious. Recruiters in forums I've read have accepted the norm of using AI to conduct interviews. It makes their jobs easier, allowing them to select the best candidates and "weed out" those on the fringes.

As rude as it sounds, the process treats you like a parasitic life-form, competing for scraps. You are forced to accept it. According to them, this is the best way to gauge the applicant's eagerness.

But since when did desperation become a requirement for getting a job? Is the ability to endure endless assessments really a measure of competence? Does being willing to jump through every hoop prove that someone will be good at their work? Or does it simply prove that they have enough energy left to keep playing the game?

## Fatigue

At first, I couldn't understand why AI was swallowing up so many roles in corporate environments, despite the hidden costs it brings through inefficient processes and inaccurate outputs. It is no longer just a tool. It is becoming a corporate machine, one that promises efficiency while quietly demanding more from the very people it was supposed to help.

I am slowly realizing why now. In this economy, we're all just surviving. We're just getting by.

We're getting used to work that is just "good enough."

And maybe that's the most unsettling part of all. We're not necessarily becoming less capable. We're becoming exhausted. When survival takes up most of our energy, excellence becomes a luxury, and even the desire to do better begins to fade.

AI isn't the only thing reshaping the workplace. It's also exposing what the workplace has already become: a system so obsessed with efficiency that it forgets the human beings keeping it running.

And somewhere between the applications, the automated interviews, and the endless demand to prove our worth, we are left wondering whether getting a job is still about finding a place where we can contribute or simply proving that we can endure the process long enough to deserve one.
