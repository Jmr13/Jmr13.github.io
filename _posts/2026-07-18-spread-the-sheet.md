### Culture
Being here for almost 3 years now in this company, it is to be expected I'd have a deep understanding of the 'work culture' prevailing in the organization. One you would notice even if you were only starting out, is the overusage of spreadsheets to whatever task or deliverables you can think of. 

You would be surprised how resourceful people are on how they use cells, the rows and columns, sometimes merging cells and adding colors to make the presentation look clean and professional. From the usuals like creating summarized reports, charts and any other visualizations, up into I would say 'bizarre', like creating prototype UI design of an application (I hope not for databases). Being the one having exposed and trying all trending tools trying to look cool back in my university (because I like clicking fancy buttons) minority of these spreadsheet's usecases are shocking, one you wouldn't imagine to do with your tasks. But they found a way anyways.

The 'spreadsheet culture' would raise resistance to people who always likes to be 'in', and some forward thinkers who ignore the old norms. It was very traditional if you look at it once and then more. Looking more at it, you will start to crave that something has to change. After careful introspection you will realize, those texts imprisoned in those tiny cells are not efficient enought to get the job done.

### Juggle

Switching to multiple tools trying to switch contexts, opening tabs, and locating the buttons you usually click is taxing. Using different tools with the help of AI is not much confusing now apparently. We're moving towards work where AI cohesively manages tasks on our behalf, AI agents simultaneously using more than 3 tools to accomplish work. It's better to use a superpowered tool, than to use a traditional and lesser-feature tool. I dare not say using multiple tools will reduce friction and tremendously boost productivity among employees. But sticking to only one tool, limits your vision.

> You cannot aim and throw a hammer to a ranged target and expect a bullseye. 
~ my former self would think.

### Infinity

Nothing is inevitable to change. As time passes by, some of the things you're looking infront of you will change it's physical attributes and your ideas will be shaped by the culture where you belong. The same can be applied to tools. Every month, updates are rolling out, you need to install the recent version, try the new features implemented. During this fleeting moment, you would be happy to experience something new.

But change would also be the reason why you will not be able to use the tool you get used to using. A frightening announcement from the developers detailing the end of life support for the tool you use will make you agitated then suffer. Next is you will search an alternative tool like a duplicate. You are lucky if you found one having the same interface or design.

The fact is, it takes time to master a tool, no matter how good tutorials are available in the internet. No matter how user-friendly it is. It's not efficient to learn and master a tool all over again and again.

In the business world, time is currency. They won't spend, if they can't double the return. Once in a while, change is forbidden if it takes too much time and when everything is working perfectly fine. 

### Constant
Text is the most unchanging form of symbol. It is like the universal language of all applications. If you wanted a file type you can keep for years and wanted it to remain as is for a long long time, without compromising versatility, you will never go wrong with a text file. 

Back to 'spreadsheet culture'. Those rows and columns are just conformity. An illusion for you to instill order. Behind those cells are just comma separated values (CSV). 

It could be easily exported to an easily readable, human understandable format. It was an already established standard instigating usage to all sorts of documentations, diagrams, UI prototypes, and whatnots.

When the norm keeps being practiced, eventually all will follow its direction. 

### Merit

I willfully accept my defeat, after coming to conclusion, that the one I'm opposing, is much more applicable to resist change, achieving eternity. Bank applications are not rewritten just because there's a trendy and fancy way to design a button. Like them, they resist change. They fully know how the 'currency' works.

Sticking and mastering a certain tool preserves all your effort and time. It also lessens the abrupt switch, once you encountered an obsolete tool. 

### King > Jack of All Trades
If I happen to run into one of my previous teammates or managers by chance because of a townmall meeting or the likes, I can always imagine them standing in an open field. Holding a hammer with squinted eyes and effortlessly aiming to a range, thousands of meters away from them, throwing it after. 

Then someone will shout "Bullseye!" 

Without fully seeing the hammer how it hits the range, I instinctively know, just by how weirdly it is positioned when it lands. 

I am certain, the claw of the hammer pierces the surface of the range.
